Awesome neural/psychological manifolds

A curated list containing neural manifold articles on top neuroscience-related journals. The list is in an alphabetical order in APA citation style. 0 means the most important and the earliest discovery.

The FAMOUS Mel Scale of Pitch in Psychoacoustics describing the neural manifold (functional, or representation-learning based, geometric objects emerged from neural population firing) of human cochlear: Stevens, S. S., Volkmann, J., & Newman, E. B. (1937). A scale for the measurement of the psychological magnitude pitch. The journal of the acoustical society of america, 8(3), 185-190.

Adams, R. A., Napier, G., Roiser, J. P., Mathys, C., & Gilleen, J. (2018). Attractor-like dynamics in belief updating in schizophrenia. Journal of neuroscience, 38(44), 9471-9485. <i>HGF, hierarchical guassian filter, Friston, active inference, attractor</i>

Barrigón, M. L., Berrouiguet, S., Carballo, J. J., Bonal-Giménez, C., Fernández-Navarro, P., Pfang, B., ... & Baca-García, E. (2017). User profiles of an electronic mental health tool for ecological momentary assessment: MEmind. International Journal of Methods in Psychiatric Research, 26(1), e1554. <i>The Spanish digital mental-health company/platform MeMind/MEmind introduced its Wellness Tracker as an ecological momentary assessment system and compared 2,838 active users with 10,973 nonusers, including differences in diagnoses, stressful events, and suicidal thoughts or behavior. The study establishes who engaged with the platform and which variables predicted use rather than testing a suicide-prediction model. In psychological-manifold terms, it supplies repeated person-level observations that could sample positions on a latent mental-health or suicide-risk manifold, but it neither estimates a manifold nor defines a psychological-distance metric.</i>

Barrigón, M. L., Romero-Medrano, L., Moreno-Muñoz, P., Porras-Segovia, A., López-Castroman, J., Courtet, P., Artés-Rodríguez, A., & Baca-García, E. (2023). One-week suicide risk prediction using real-time smartphone monitoring: Prospective cohort study. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 25, e43719. <i>Researchers in the Spanish MeMind/MEmind network combined real-time smartphone monitoring with passive behavioral features to predict a suicide-risk event during the following week, reporting prospective discrimination from changes in mobility, time at home, and app-use patterns. This is a digital-phenotyping prediction study rather than a language or treatment study. In psychological-manifold terms, the behavioral features may be interpreted as coordinates and short-term movement through a latent risk state space, but the paper uses predictive modeling rather than explicit manifold learning, curvature, or geodesic distance.</i>

Barrigón, M. L., Schmidt, C., Elices, M., Porras-Segovia, A., De Granda-Beltrán, A. M., Artés-Rodríguez, A., Courtet, P., Pérez-Sola, V., & Baca-García, E. (2025). Impact of digital safety plan activation on subsequent emergency department visits following an initial suicide attempt: Quasi-experimental study. JMIR Mental Health, 12, e70253. <i>The Spanish digital mental-health company/platform MeMind/MEmind delivered a clinician-configured digital safety plan to people presenting after suicidal behavior; participants who activated the plan during later crises had a lower likelihood of another suicide-related emergency-department visit, although activation was self-selected. This is the series’ clearest treatment-oriented paper, but its quasi-experimental design does not establish randomized causal efficacy. In psychological-manifold terms, the safety plan can be viewed as an intervention intended to redirect a person’s trajectory away from a high-risk region, yet the study does not estimate that state-space geometry or measure movement along it.</i>

Boccato, T., Testolin, A., & Zorzi, M. (2021). Learning numerosity representations with transformers: Number generation tasks and out-of-distribution generalization. Entropy, 23(7), 857. <i>Series paper of disentangled representation. A pixel-level Transformer learns a conditionally disentangled representation of numerosity and generates dot images containing both trained and previously unseen numerosities. Its main contribution is demonstrating that an approximate cardinal-number geometry can support interpolation and extrapolation beyond the training distribution. For clinical psychology, the takeaway is that continuous clinical constructs could be explicitly conditioned, disentangled from nuisance factors, and evaluated through out-of-distribution generalization rather than only in-sample prediction.</i>

Boyle, L. M., Posani, L., Irfan, S., Siegelbaum, S. A., & Fusi, S. (2024). Tuned geometries of hippocampal representations meet the computational demands of social memory. Neuron, 112(8), 1358–1371.e9. <i>Novel conspecifics were represented by a relatively low-dimensional geometry supporting generalization across identity and location, whereas familiar conspecifics were represented in a higher-dimensional geometry supporting individuated, high-capacity memory. Individual variation in this dimensional expansion predicted social-recognition performance. For psychometrics, the central lesson is that lower dimensionality is not automatically better: compressed geometry supports abstraction and transfer, while expanded geometry can support specificity and reduced interference. The appropriate geometry therefore depends on the psychological function that the construct must perform.</i>

Busch, E. L., Conley, M. I., & Baskin-Sommers, A. (2025). Manifold learning uncovers nonlinear interactions between the adolescent brain and environment that predict emotional and behavioral problems. Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging, 10(5), 463-474. <i>Novel, using manifold to model the interaction between dimensions and contexts. EPHATE git repo</i>

Busch, E. L., Fincke, E. C., Lajoie, G., Krishnaswamy, S., & Turk-Browne, N. B. (2026). Human learning of noninvasive brain–computer interfaces via manifold geometry. Nature Neuroscience. Advance online publication. <i>Strong causal evidence that intrinsic manifold geometry constrains human learning. Participants learned real-time fMRI brain–computer-interface mappings when the required changes followed high-variance directions of their existing neural manifold, but not when mappings required off-manifold activity. For longitudinal and clinical psychometrics, this suggests that change may be easier along directions already available in a person’s local tangent space. A treatment target could therefore be psychologically close for one patient but effectively off-manifold for another, although this clinical interpretation still requires direct testing.</i>

Caspi, A., Houts, R. M., Fisher, H. L., Danese, A., & Moffitt, T. E. (2024). The general factor of psychopathology (p): Choosing among competing models and interpreting p. Clinical Psychological Science, 12(1), 53-82. <i>positive manifold of psychopathology/clinical psychology, the p factor, which is just the highest construct in the hierarchy of Kotov's HiTOP system</i>

Chaudhuri, R., Gerçek, B., Pandey, B., Peyrache, A., & Fiete, I. (2019). The intrinsic attractor manifold and population dynamics of a canonical cognitive circuit across waking and sleep. Nature neuroscience, 22(9), 1512-1520.

Chung, S., & Abbott, L. F. (2021). Neural population geometry: An approach for understanding biological and artificial neural networks. Current opinion in neurobiology, 70, 137-144. <i>This is the review paper of the manifold approach on neural populations (near 1K neurons or more) on the top review journal in biology targeted both for neuroscientists and deep learning researchers.</i>

Clapp Sullivan, M. L., Schwaba, T., Harden, K. P., Grotzinger, A. D., Nivard, M. G., & Tucker-Drob, E. M. (2024). Beyond the factor indeterminacy problem using genome-wide association data. Nature human behaviour, 8(2), 205-218. <i>the positive manifold of intelligence, the g factor</i>

Constantinescu, A. O., O’Reilly, J. X., & Behrens, T. E. (2016). Organizing conceptual knowledge in humans with a gridlike code. Science, 352(6292), 1464-1468. 

Courellis, H. S., Minxha, J., Cardenas, A. R., Kimmel, D. L., Reed, C. M., Valiante, T. A., ... & Rutishauser, U. (2024). Abstract representations emerge in human hippocampal neurons during inference. Nature, 632(8026), 841–849. <i>Single-neuron recordings in humans showed that learning to make inferences reorganized hippocampal population activity into an abstract, disentangled representational geometry. The important result is not merely low dimensionality: parallel and separable coding directions supported generalization to new combinations of task variables. For geometric psychometrics, this provides a functional interpretation of construct validity: convergent validity may involve preservation of a shared direction, discriminant validity may involve separable directions, and generalizability may depend on whether these directions remain stable across contexts.</i>

Darshan, R., & Rivkind, A. (2022). Learning to represent continuous variables in heterogeneous neural networks. Cell Reports, 39(1).

Fascianelli, V., Battista, A., Stefanini, F., Tsujimoto, S., Genovesio, A., & Fusi, S. (2024). Neural representational geometries reflect behavioral differences in monkeys and recurrent neural networks. Nature Communications, 15, 6479. <i>Two monkeys achieved broadly similar task performance while exhibiting markedly different prefrontal representational geometries; subsequent reaction-time analyses and recurrent neural-network models indicated different task strategies. This is a particularly clear psychometric warning that the same total score can arise from different latent organizations. Person-specific geometry may therefore distinguish strategy, compensation, and likely future behavior that are invisible to sum scores. The biological sample was only two monkeys, so the study is an existence proof rather than population-level individual-differences evidence.</i>

Ferrante, M., Boccato, T., Ozcelik, F., VanRullen, R., & Toschi, N. (2024). Through their eyes: Multi-subject brain decoding with simple alignment techniques. Imaging Neuroscience, 2, 1–21. <i>Series paper of disentangled representation. This study aligns one participant’s fMRI activity with another participant’s functional space and then applies a decoder trained on the reference participant. Its main contribution is showing that simple ridge-regression alignment using approximately 10% shared stimuli can approach within-subject decoding performance while greatly reducing subject-specific data requirements. For clinical psychology, the takeaway is that patient-specific alignment layers may support a shared group-level neural manifold without assuming that all patients have identical neural representations.</i>

Ferrante, M., Boccato, T., Passamonti, L., & Toschi, N. (2024). Retrieving and reconstructing conceptually similar images from fMRI with latent diffusion models and a neuro-inspired brain decoding model. Journal of Neural Engineering, 21(4), 046001. <i>Series paper of disentangled representation. This study linearly maps fMRI activity into the high-level latent space of a pretrained visual network and uses nearest-neighbor retrieval and latent diffusion to recover conceptually similar images. Its main contribution is demonstrating across three fMRI datasets that neural measurements can be aligned with a shared semantic latent space and used for generative reconstruction. For clinical psychology, the takeaway is that patient neural signals could potentially be projected into clinically interpretable semantic spaces, although psychiatric validity and cross-patient generalization require direct empirical testing.</i>

Or, one could say influence each other.</i>

Genkin, M., Shenoy, K. V., Chandrasekaran, C., & Engel, T. A. (2025). The dynamics and geometry of choice in the premotor cortex. Nature, 645(8079), 168–176. <i>The study jointly inferred a nonlinear population geometry and an attractor-like flow field for single-trial choice formation in primate premotor cortex. Its major conceptual contribution is separating the geometry of representation from the dynamics of movement through that geometry. For geometric psychometrics, a patient’s location on a symptom manifold is therefore insufficient: velocity, direction, attractors, and barriers must also be estimated. Cross-sectional geometry alone cannot identify whether two people at the same symptom level are improving, deteriorating, or dynamically stable.</i>

Jones, S. H., Thornicroft, G., Coffey, M., & Dunn, G. (1995). A brief mental health outcome scale: Reliability and validity of the Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF). Jurewicz, K., Sleezer, B. J., Mehta, P. S., Hayden, B. Y., & Ebitz, R. B. (2024). Irrational choices via a curvilinear representational geometry for value. Nature Communications, 15, 6424. <i>Value representations in macaque ventromedial prefrontal cortex followed a curved rather than linear manifold, and this curvature predicted a specific decoy-induced violation of rational choice that was observed behaviorally. This is unusually strong evidence that curvature itself can be psychologically consequential. In psychometrics, it implies that equal coordinate differences need not represent equal psychological distances and that locally adequate linear models may fail globally. The limitation is that this is a task-specific value manifold in two macaques, not yet a general demonstration for personality, intelligence, or psychopathology.</i>

Kang, W., Kwon, M., & Lee, S.-H. (2025). Neural systems for processing social relationship information along two principal dimensions. Communications Biology, 8, 1120. <i>Naturalistic social-interaction ratings yielded two approximately orthogonal dimensions, including an amity–hostility dimension, with partially distinct posterior and anterior cortical systems. This is relevant to the interpersonal circumplex because it supports low-dimensional geometric organization of social perception. It does not by itself validate a circumplex: a stronger test would require circular ordering, expected angular positions, appropriate spacing, interpretable radius, and invariance across people, cultures, and situations. The interpersonal circumplex remains an unusually strong test case because it specifies its geometry a priori.</i>

Keller, A. S., Pines, A. R., Shanmugan, S., Sydnor, V. J., Cui, Z., Bertolero, M. A., ... & Satterthwaite, T. D. (2023). Personalized functional brain network topography is associated with individual differences in youth cognition. Nature Communications, 14, 8411. <i>Across matched discovery and replication samples totaling 6,972 children, individualized functional-network topography predicted general cognition in held-out data, especially in association networks along the sensorimotor-to-association hierarchy. This supports the importance of person-specific spatial organization for the positive manifold of intelligence. However, it does not establish that intelligence itself has a nonlinear manifold: the findings are also compatible with a general factor, overlapping networks, developmental coupling, or other models. Its strongest contribution is showing that individual geometry should not automatically be treated as nuisance variation.</i>

Kim, S. S., Rouault, H., Druckmann, S., & Jayaraman, V. (2017). Ring attractor dynamics in the Drosophila central brain. Science, 356(6340), 849-853.

Kim, Y. H., Jang, J., Kang, N., Jeong, J. H., Kim, J., Ahn, Y. M., ... & Kim, S. H. (2025). Revealing differential psychotic symptoms in schizophrenia and bipolar I disorder by manifold learning and network analyses. Translational Psychiatry, 15(1), 194. <i>One of the closest direct applications of manifold learning to psychopathology. UMAP and symptom-network analyses showed different organizations of lifetime psychotic symptoms across schizophrenia-spectrum and bipolar I disorders, suggesting that diagnostic categories and linear totals may conceal clinically relevant heterogeneity. From a psychometric perspective, however, the result remains exploratory: UMAP geometry can change with distance metrics, hyperparameters, symptom prevalence, and sample composition. A convincing psychopathology manifold would require out-of-sample replication and incremental prediction of course, treatment response, or transitions beyond factor, mixture, and network models.</i>

Lemey, C., Larsen, M. E., Devylder, J., Courtet, P., Billot, R., Lenca, P., Walter, M., Baca-García, E., & Berrouiguet, S. (2019). Clinicians’ concerns about mobile ecological momentary assessment tools designed for emerging psychiatric problems: Prospective acceptability assessment of the MEmind app. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 21(4), e10111. <i>This paper evaluates clinicians’ acceptance of the Spanish MeMind/MEmind platform for collecting clinical data and ecological momentary assessments, identifying practical concerns about workflow, usefulness, and implementation. Its basic contribution is operational: it examines whether repeated real-world measurement can be embedded in psychiatric care. In psychological-manifold terms, reliable clinical adoption is a prerequisite for densely sampling psychological states across contexts and time, but the paper itself contains no manifold model or distance construction.</i>

Lin, X.-X., Nieder, A., & Jacob, S. N. (2023). The neuronal implementation of representational geometry in primate prefrontal cortex. Science Advances, 9(50), eadh8685. <i>This study decomposed population-level representational geometry into sparse, biologically interpretable components linked to small neuronal subpopulations, revealing working-memory representations and post-distraction recovery signals missed by conventional analyses. For geometric psychometrics, the analogous requirement is indicator-level traceability: global manifold shape should be attributable to particular items, linguistic features, behaviors, contexts, or time segments. Without such decomposition, a geometric model risks becoming an attractive but opaque embedding rather than an interpretable measurement model.</i>

Nour, M. M., Liu, Y., El-Gaby, M., McCutcheon, R. A., & Dolan, R. J. (2025). Cognitive maps and schizophrenia. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 29(2), 184-200. <i>Schizophrenia and neural manifolds.</i>

O’Neill, P.-K., Posani, L., Meszaros, J., Warren, P., Schoonover, C. E., Fink, A. J. P., Fusi, S., & Salzman, C. D. (2026). The representational geometry of emotional states in basolateral amygdala. The study primarily evaluates recruitment, retention, compliance, and smartphone-use patterns, showing that multimodal monitoring is feasible in real-world mental-health settings. In psychological-manifold terms, active symptoms and passive behavior provide complementary observation channels for reconstructing a latent psychological state space, but feasibility alone does not establish its topology, metric, or invariance across groups.</i>

Porras-Segovia, A., Díaz-Oliván, I., Barrigón, M. L., Moreno, M., Artés-Rodríguez, A., Pérez-Rodríguez, M. M., & Baca-García, E. (2022). In psychological-manifold terms, longitudinal dropout creates nonuniform and potentially biased sampling of the risk manifold, so any inferred geometry or trajectories would need explicit missingness and selection modeling.</i>

Porras-Segovia, A., Moreno, M., Barrigón, M. L., López-Castroman, J., Courtet, P., Berrouiguet, S., Artés-Rodríguez, A., & Baca-García, E. (2023). In psychological-manifold terms, these differences suggest potentially distinct observable regions or trajectories within suicidality, but the paper does not demonstrate that the phenotypes lie on a stable common manifold.</i>

Its importance is conceptual rather than empirical, and it remains non-peer-reviewed.
