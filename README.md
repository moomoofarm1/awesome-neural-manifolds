Natural language processing of youth speech predicts psychopathology across adolescence. Nature Mental Health, 1-12.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-026-00683-9) <i>Geometric interpretation of a risk got from sentence embeddings. The keyword is the projection of the regression weights to a 1-dim risk construct, which is exactly the math concept of the Affine transform/projection. The paper projects sentence embeddings onto a coefficient vector learned by a covariate-adjusted model, but the sentence score itself excludes demographic terms and is not a strict additive contribution to the full prediction. It is therefore better understood as a post hoc ranking of sentences along a model-derived linguistic risk direction, rather than an adjusted risk estimate. In psychological-manifold terms, the coefficient vector defines one supervised axis through the embedding space, and each sentence receives a coordinate along that axis. 8. [Boyle, L. M., Posani, L., Irfan, S., Siegelbaum, S. A., & Fusi, S. (2024). Tuned geometries of hippocampal representations meet the computational demands of social memory. For psychometrics, the central lesson is that lower dimensionality is not automatically better: compressed geometry supports abstraction and transfer, while expanded geometry can support specificity and reduced interference. The appropriate geometry therefore depends on the psychological function that the construct must perform.</i>

In psychological-manifold terms, active symptoms and passive behavior provide complementary observation channels for reconstructing a latent psychological state space, but feasibility alone does not establish its topology, metric, or invariance across groups.</i>
52. [Porras-Segovia, A., Díaz-Oliván, I., Barrigón, M. L., Moreno, M., Artés-Rodríguez, A., Pérez-Rodríguez, M. M., & Baca-García, E. (2022). Real-world feasibility and acceptability of real-time suicide risk monitoring via smartphones: A 6-month follow-up cohort. Journal of Psychiatric Research, 149, 145-154.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2022.02.026) <i>The Spanish MeMind/MEmind research programme followed 393 people at high risk of suicide with active MEmind assessments and passive eB2 monitoring for six months, quantifying retention, satisfaction, and factors associated with discontinuation. The central finding is that both modes are feasible but engagement declines substantially, especially for active assessment. In psychological-manifold terms, longitudinal dropout creates nonuniform and potentially biased sampling of the risk manifold, so any inferred geometry or trajectories would need explicit missingness and selection modeling.</i>
53. [Porras-Segovia, A., Moreno, M., Barrigón, M. L., López-Castroman, J., Courtet, P., Berrouiguet, S., Artés-Rodríguez, A., & Baca-García, E. (2023). Six-month clinical and ecological momentary assessment follow-up of patients at high risk of suicide: A survival analysis. Journal of Clinical Psychiatry, 84(1), 22m14411.](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.22m14411) <i>Using the Spanish MeMind/MEmind app, this cohort study combined six-month clinical follow-up with smartphone ecological momentary assessment in people with previous suicidal behavior and tested which variables were associated with later clinical or EMA-defined suicidal events. Extreme EMA responses were associated with clinical suicidal events over two months but not clearly over six months, and the authors reported differing correlates for EMA and clinical events. In psychological-manifold terms, these differences suggest potentially distinct observable regions or trajectories within suicidality, but the paper does not demonstrate that the phenotypes lie on a stable common manifold.</i>
54. [Ramsay, J. O., Li, J., Wallmark, J., & Wiberg, M. (2025). An information manifold perspective for analyzing test data. Applied psychological measurement, 49(3), 90-108.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/01466216241310600) <i>one-dimesional manifold based on surprise from Swedish SAT test, and more forms of manifolds</i>
55. [Sedano-Capdevila, A., Toledo-Acosta, M., Barrigón, M. L., Morales-González, E., Torres-Moreno, D., Martínez-Zaldivar, B., Hermosillo-Valadez, J., & Baca-García, E. (2023). Text mining methods for the characterisation of suicidal thoughts and behaviour. Psychiatry Research, 322, 115090.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115090) <i>The Spanish MeMind/MEmind Study Group applied natural-language-processing methods to short patient texts and classified self-reported lack of desire to live, while examining which words differentiated risk groups; the reported ROC-AUC was 0.964. The paper operationalizes suicidal cognition through language and extends the earlier geometric embedding work into a more conventional predictive text-mining pipeline. In psychological-manifold terms, the text representation defines a latent decision geometry related to suicidality, but it does not independently validate distances between psychological states or establish a Riemannian manifold.</i>
56. [Sohn, H., Narain, D., Meirhaeghe, N., & Jazayeri, M. (2019). Bayesian computation through cortical latent dynamics. Neuron, 103(5), 934-947.](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(19)30562-8)
57. [Stevens, S. S. (1946). On the theory of scales of measurement. Science, 103(2684), 677-680.](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.103.2684.677) <i>Almost the creator of psychological measurement theories in early psychometrics from sensation and perception, psychophysics. The theory of Levels of Measurement is widely cited in behavioral statistics and psychometrics, esp. in basic introductory textbooks.</i>
58. [Thornton, M. A., Rmus, M., Vyas, A. D., & Tamir, D. I. (2023). Transition dynamics shape mental state concepts. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 152(10), 2804.](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2023-66914-001) <i>psychological manifold in experimental psychology; transition is just akin to the phase transition in thermodynamics/statistical physics.</i>
59. [Toledo-Acosta, M., Barreiro, T., Reig-Alamillo, A., Müller, M., Aroca Bisquert, F., Barrigón, M. L., Baca-García, E., & Hermosillo-Valadez, J. (2020). Cognitive emotional embedded representations of text to predict suicidal ideation and psychiatric symptoms. Mathematics, 8(11), 2088.](https://doi.org/10.3390/math8112088) <i>Researchers associated with the Spanish digital mental-health company/platform MeMind/MEmind published the closest match to a “suicide manifold” paper: it transforms word2vec representations with cognitively based emotional scores and introduces topological-geometrical clustering for sparsely distributed high-dimensional patient texts. The method explores word associations, emotional polarity, and embedding geometry and proposes potential use in predicting lack of desire to live and preventing suicide, without prospective clinical validation. Relative to the psychological-manifold concept, this is a direct candidate language manifold because it explicitly constructs a geometric/topological representation, but it does not define a Riemannian metric, estimate geodesic psychological distance, or independently verify that its distances correspond to psychological distances. The most foundational and methodological paper by the company working on suicide risk.</i>
60. [Trope, Y., & Liberman, N. (2010). Construal-level theory of psychological distance. Psychological review, 117(2), 440.](https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2010-06891-005.html)<i>psychoogical distance</i>
61. [Tuerlinckx, F. (2024). A geometrical perspective on parametric psychometric models. arXiv preprint arXiv:2410.12450.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12450) <i>The key theoretical paper directly connecting non-Euclidean geometry with psychometrics. It treats a parametric family of probability distributions as a statistical manifold equipped with the Fisher-information metric and discusses invariant distance, curvature, volume, and a geodesic ability measure for item-response models. Its importance is conceptual rather than empirical, and it remains non-peer-reviewed. It establishes the geometry of psychometric models, not yet that people, symptoms, or item-response profiles occupy a stable nonlinear phenotypic manifold.</i>
62. [Vinograd, A., Nair, A., Kim, J. H., Linderman, S. W., & Anderson, D. J. (2024). Causal evidence of a line attractor encoding an affective state. Nature, 634(8035), 910-918.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07915-x)

64. [Wakhloo, A. J., Slatton, W., & Chung, S. (2026). Neural population geometry and optimal coding of tasks with shared latent structure. Nature Neuroscience, 29, 682–692.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02183-y) <i>The paper derives geometric statistics linking neural representations to out-of-sample generalization across tasks sharing latent structure, and supports the theory with biological and artificial neural data. Early learning favored lower-dimensional, more correlated representations, whereas later learning produced more differentiated and factorized codes. This offers a bridge from descriptive geometry to measurement theory: dimensionality, signal factorization, and correlation structure could become psychometric estimands explaining why measures with similar reliability differ in transfer. Their sampling properties and measurement invariance are not yet established.</i>

Employing contemporary integrative interpersonal theory to understand dysfunction in those at clinical high risk for psychosis. ## Psychological manifolds originated from psychophysics

> **Historical note.** “Psychological manifold” is used here retrospectively. Early researchers spoke of psychophysical scales, psychological or perceptual spaces, multidimensional scaling, and representational geometry. Psychophysics itself predates Wundt: Fechner formalized the field, while Wundt helped institutionalize quantitative experimental psychology.

0. [Fechner, G. T. (1860). *Elemente der Psychophysik*. Leipzig: Breitkopf und Härtel.](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15467887W/Elemente_der_Psychophysik) <i>Foundational psychophysics: physical stimulation and subjective sensation were treated as quantities connected by lawful transformations. This is the one-dimensional ancestor of later psychological geometry.</i>
1. [Wundt, W. (1874). *Grundzüge der physiologischen Psychologie*. Leipzig: W. Engelmann.](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25659452M/Grundzuge_der_physiologischen_Psychologie) <i>Foundational source for experimental psychology. Wundt made controlled measurement of sensation, perception, attention, and reaction time central to the new laboratory discipline; his Leipzig institute followed in 1879.</i>
2. [Thurstone, L. L. (1927). A law of comparative judgment. *Psychological Review, 34*(4), 273–286.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070288) <i>Converted pairwise comparative judgments into positions on a latent psychological scale, an important bridge from psychophysics to latent-space measurement.</i>
3. [Stevens, S. S., Volkmann, J., & Newman, E. B. (1937). A scale for the measurement of the psychological magnitude pitch. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 8*(3), 185–190.](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1915893) <i>The Mel scale showed that physical frequency and subjective pitch use different coordinates. It is an early psychophysical scaling result, not by itself evidence of a neural manifold.</i>
4. [MacAdam, D. L. (1942). Visual sensitivities to color differences in daylight. *Journal of the Optical Society of America, 32*(5), 247–274.](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.32.000247) <i>MacAdam ellipses showed that local discriminability in color space is direction-dependent. Retrospectively, they resemble measurements of a nonuniform local perceptual metric.</i>
5. [Torgerson, W. S. (1952). Multidimensional scaling: I. Theory and method. *Psychometrika, 17*(4), 401–419.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288916) <i>Formalized multidimensional scaling: psychological coordinates can be reconstructed from observed dissimilarities rather than specified in advance.</i>
6. [Stevens, S. S. (1957). On the psychophysical law. *Psychological Review, 64*(3), 153–181.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046162) <i>Stevens's power law generalized direct psychophysical scaling and emphasized that subjective magnitude can be a nonlinear transformation of physical magnitude.</i>
7. [Indow, T., & Uchizono, T. (1960). Multidimensional mapping of Munsell colors varying in hue and chroma. *Journal of Experimental Psychology, 59*, 321–329.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0049149) <i>An early empirical reconstruction of a multidimensional color-perception space from psychological judgments.</i>
8. [Shepard, R. N. (1962). The analysis of proximities: Multidimensional scaling with an unknown distance function. I. *Psychometrika, 27*(2), 125–140.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289630) <i>Nonmetric MDS made it possible to recover a psychological geometry from ordinal proximity relations even when the mapping from judged proximity to distance was unknown.</i>
9. [Shepard, R. N. (1964). Circularity in judgments of relative pitch. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 36*(12), 2346–2353.](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1919362) <i>Pitch-class judgments revealed a circular structure that cannot be represented adequately by a single rectilinear axis, an early example of nontrivial perceptual topology.</i>
10. [Kruskal, J. B. (1964). Multidimensional scaling by optimizing goodness of fit to a nonmetric hypothesis. *Psychometrika, 29*(1), 1–27.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289565) <i>Introduced the stress-based optimization framework that made nonmetric MDS a practical method for recovering psychological spaces.</i>
11. [Shepard, R. N., & Chipman, S. (1970). Second-order isomorphism of internal representations: Shapes of states. *Cognitive Psychology, 1*(1), 1–17.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(70)90002-2) <i>Argued that internal representations need not resemble objects directly; instead, relations among internal representations can preserve relations among external objects. This is a conceptual foundation of representational geometry.</i>
12. [Grey, J. M. (1977). Multidimensional perceptual scaling of musical timbres. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 61*(5), 1270–1277.](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.381428) <i>Recovered a low-dimensional timbre space from similarity judgments and related its axes to interpretable acoustic properties.</i>
13. [Tversky, A. (1977). Features of similarity. *Psychological Review, 84*(4), 327–352.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.4.327) <i>A decisive warning against assuming ordinary metric geometry: human similarity can be asymmetric and context dependent. Psychological geometry must therefore be tested rather than imposed.</i>
14. [Russell, J. A. (1980). A circumplex model of affect. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 39*(6), 1161–1178.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077714) <i>Organized affect in a circular two-dimensional space, establishing one of psychology's best-known explicitly geometric construct models.</i>
15. [Nosofsky, R. M. (1986). Attention, similarity, and the identification–categorization relationship. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 115*(1), 39–61.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.115.1.39) <i>Showed that attention weights stretch or compress dimensions of psychological space, making effective psychological distance task dependent.</i>
16. [Shepard, R. N. (1987). Toward a universal law of generalization for psychological science. *Science, 237*(4820), 1317–1323.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.3629243) <i>Linked psychological distance to an independent behavioral outcome: generalization falls approximately exponentially with distance in an appropriate psychological space. This is a key validation principle for psychological geometry. Foundational paper 1 of psychological manifold. 心理流形奠基论文1.</i>
17. [Valentine, T. (1991). A unified account of the effects of distinctiveness, inversion, and race in face recognition. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology Section A, 43*(2), 161–204.](https://doi.org/10.1080/14640749108400966) <i>Established the influential multidimensional face-space framework, in which identity and distinctiveness are understood through locations relative to the population of represented faces.</i>
18. [Edelman, S. (1998). Representation is representation of similarities. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 21*(4), 449–467.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X98001253) <i>Developed the idea that the relational structure among representations—not a privileged coordinate system—is central to mental representation.</i>
19. [Kriegeskorte, N., Mur, M., & Bandettini, P. A. (2008). Representational similarity analysis—connecting the branches of systems neuroscience. *Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience, 2*, 4.](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.06.004.2008) <i>Made representational geometry a common comparison space for behavior, neural activity, and computational models through representational dissimilarity matrices.</i>
20. [Kriegeskorte, N., & Kievit, R. A. (2013). Representational geometry: Integrating cognition, computation, and the brain. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 17*(8), 401–412.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2013.06.007) <i>Explicitly framed representational geometry as a bridge among cognitive theory, computational models, and brain representations.</i>
21. [Schurgin, M. W., Wixted, J. T., & Brady, T. F. (2020). Psychophysical scaling reveals a unified theory of visual memory strength. *Nature Human Behaviour, 4*, 1156–1172.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-00938-0) <i>Showed that visual memory and perception are better described in a nonlinear psychophysical similarity space than in the raw stimulus coordinates, reconnecting modern memory theory to psychophysical scaling.</i>
22. [Hebart, M. N., Zheng, C. Y., Pereira, F., & Baker, C. I. (2020). Revealing the multidimensional mental representations of natural objects underlying human similarity judgements. *Nature Human Behaviour, 4*, 1173–1185.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-00951-3) <i>Recovered a large, interpretable multidimensional object space from human similarity judgments over natural objects, demonstrating psychological-space estimation at modern scale.</i>
23. [Waraich, S. A., & Victor, J. D. (2024). The geometry of low- and high-level perceptual spaces. *Journal of Neuroscience, 44*(4), e1460232023.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1460-23.2023) <i>Moved beyond ordinary embeddings by explicitly testing dimensionality and curvature across perceptual domains; higher-level semantic spaces showed stronger hierarchical or tree-like organization.</i>
24. [Marjieh, R., Jacoby, N., Peterson, J. C., & Griffiths, T. L. (2024). The universal law of generalization holds for naturalistic stimuli. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 153*(3), 573–589.](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001533) <i>Extended Shepard's distance–generalization law to large, naturalistic, high-dimensional image sets using hundreds of thousands of human judgments.</i>
25. [Roads, B. D., & Love, B. C. (2024a). Modeling similarity and psychological space. *Annual Review of Psychology, 75*, 215–240.](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-040323-115131) <i>A modern synthesis of the field: similarity and representation are treated as dual descriptions of psychological space, linking classical MDS to contemporary embeddings and representational analysis. Foundational paper 2 of psychological manifold. 心理流形奠基论文2.</i>
26. [Roads, B. D., & Love, B. C. (2024b). The dimensions of dimensionality. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 28(12), 1118-1131.](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/fulltext/S1364-6613(24)00189-X) <i>A methodological bridge from observable data to latent psychological manifolds: it distinguishes observable dimensions from inferred latent representational spaces and emphasizes that the recovered dimensionality and geometry depend jointly on the input data and embedding procedure, rather than revealing a uniquely “true” psychological space. Particularly important for a multilevel manifold framework, it shows that apparently different representational forms—such as multidimensional spaces and graphs—can encode the same relational structure, with hierarchical graphs efficiently embedded in hyperbolic space; it further formalizes cross-space correspondence through second-order isomorphism, where relational structure can agree despite different coordinates or dimensionalities. 心理流形奠基论文3.</i>
26. [Mahner, F. P., Muttenthaler, L., Güçlü, U., et al. (2025). Dimensions underlying the representational alignment of deep neural networks with humans. *Nature Machine Intelligence, 7*, 848–859.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01041-7) <i>Compared human and artificial representations through interpretable latent dimensions rather than a single global similarity score, clarifying which parts of representational geometry align across systems.</i>
27. [Muttenthaler, L., Greff, K., Born, F., et al. (2025). Aligning machine and human visual representations across abstraction levels. *Nature, 647*, 349–355.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09631-6) <i>Showed that human conceptual geometry is organized across levels of abstraction and that injecting this structure into vision models improves human alignment and generalization.</i>
28. [Seiler, J. P.-H., Cazzetta, G., Ghobadi, A., et al. (2026). Auditory perceptual maps in humans and mice share common structures and predict perceptual decisions in discrimination learning. *Communications Psychology, 4*, 98.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-026-00485-w) <i>A 2026 continuation of the psychophysical-space tradition: auditory perceptual maps recovered from behavior show shared structure across humans and mice and predict later perceptual decisions.</i>

The conceptual progression across these works is:

**physical stimulus → subjective scale → psychological distance → multidimensional psychological space → representational geometry → candidate psychological manifold**
